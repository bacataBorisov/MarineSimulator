# M3.4.0 — Physics and measurement boundaries

Status: **historical M3.4.0 step contract; measurement overlay (`ExperimentSensorModel` / `ExperimentInstrumentRuntime`) and CSV export are now wired for prepared experiments.**

Sensor availability affects measurements and output, never physical truth or vessel dynamics, unless an explicitly modeled closed-loop controller requires sensor input. No such controller exists.

## What already varies

The operator session redraws wind, compass heading, depth, sea temperature, and manual boat speed once per second. The sample is uniform across `center ± offset`. That sample is stored as `EnvironmentTruth` or as the vessel's magnetic heading. It is environmental variation, not an instrument error layered on top of a fixed truth.

`speed.offset` is a physical penalty on estimated speed: `max(0.88, 1 - offset / 100)`. It is not a speed-log measurement error.

Live-weather wind wander and the wire-fault stage are separate. Live weather is unseeded. Wire faults run after a sentence exists, default off, and do not read vessel truth.

## What a reading is today

`SimulationSnapshot` copies the instrument fields after the tick. Sentence builders read that snapshot. The dashboard later receives the same fields. There is no second measurement record.

Disabling the depth sounder clears the depth reading and the DBT sentence. Position, heading, wind truth, and speed through water stay on the same track.

Every instrument switch controls the published reading only. The 1 Hz wind, sea temperature, heading, depth, and manual-speed samples are stored whether or not that instrument is on. Estimated speed reads the wind truth. Steering reads the vessel heading. Position keeps integrating. Air, humidity, and pressure are drawn from a separate generator so enabling them does not change the wind, heading, depth, or speed sequence.

While a sensor is off, its snapshot fields and its sentences stay empty. Turning it back on publishes the current physical value. GNSS and DSC have no simulation effect.

## What may affect physics

- The 1 Hz wind redraw, because estimated speed reads it.
- The 1 Hz heading redraw, because it becomes the steered heading.
- The manual speed redraw, because it becomes speed through water.
- `speed.offset`, as the estimated-speed penalty.
- The hidden polar, when a scenario sets one. The reference polar does not move the boat.

## What must not affect physics later

A future bias, noise, delay, or dropout written for an experiment may change the snapshot and the sentence. It must not change latitude, longitude, steered heading, speed through water, speed over ground, course over ground, or `EnvironmentTruth`.

Wire faults and the instrument switches meet that rule. The heading redraw does not, because that redraw is the steered heading.

## Limits

The scenario runner steadies the wind by clearing the wind offset. A heading offset on the baseline still redraws the steered heading from the scenario seed. Compass deviation is a published curve, not a selected error. GPS position has no measurement error. A zero boat speed still suppresses apparent wind.

The voyage-log counters pause while the speed log is off. The track does not. Starting a tack still requires an anemometer and a heading instrument; a maneuver already in progress does not consult those switches. Fetching a new live-weather forecast still requires GPS. A forecast already stored is applied with the GPS switch off. MWD and VPW stay suppressed unless the anemometer, a speed source, and a heading source are all on. That is sentence availability. The snapshot still carries the vessel coordinate when GPS is off, and the GPS sentences are omitted.
